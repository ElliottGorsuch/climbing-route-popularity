A sample, not a census

The main dataset inner-joins Kaggle features and archived bouldering metadata with route counts derived from the Georgia Tech research archive. The archive includes 2,115,034 records for 47,002 users, capped at 1,000 records per user. Individual dates and tick IDs are absent. Some ticks may record attempts.

Absolute tick records are the primary outcome. Distinct sampled climbers are a sensitivity measure. Repeated rows cannot reliably distinguish repeat climbs from collection duplicates.

One route, one style

Routes flagged ice, aid or snow are excluded from these rock comparisons and remain in the source dataset and notebook. Among the rest, any trad flag wins; otherwise bouldering takes precedence over sport. Other rock and top-rope-only routes are documented separately.

No five-tick minimum is applied. Grades are compared within their own scales. Coarse families preserve 5.10 as text, never as the decimal 5.1. Archive-supported resolutions of ambiguous source 5.1 values are documented in the notebook.

Sources & reproducibility

Source code, data definitions, audit tables and the executed notebook are in the public repository. The original tick archive specifies no data license; our MIT code license does not license those original records. The explorer includes route features and aggregate counts only.

What regression can tell us

Models examine log(1 + recorded sample ticks), with grade-family and state controls and climbing-area-clustered uncertainty. Roped and bouldering models remain separate. Results concern associations within observed routes, not causal effects, future traffic or route safety.

Missing features, historical/newer snapshot differences, unknown route age and sample selection limit conclusions. The notebook contains coefficients, diagnostics and sensitivity analyses.
