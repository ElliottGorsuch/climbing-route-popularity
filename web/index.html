Nationwide scope, with matched routes in 48 of 50 states. Louisiana and Nebraska have no retained routes in this dataset. Alaska and Hawaii are included. Missing coverage is not evidence of no climbing.

A sample, not a census

The main dataset inner-joins Kaggle features and archived bouldering metadata with route counts derived from the Georgia Tech research archive. The archive includes 2,115,034 records for 47,002 users, capped at 1,000 records per user. Individual dates and tick IDs are absent. Some ticks may record attempts.

Absolute tick records are the primary outcome. Distinct sampled climbers are a sensitivity measure. Repeated rows cannot reliably distinguish repeat climbs from collection duplicates.

One route, one style

Routes flagged ice, aid or snow are excluded from these rock comparisons and remain in the source dataset and notebook. Among the rest, any trad flag wins; otherwise bouldering takes precedence over sport. Other rock and top-rope-only routes are documented separately.

No five-tick minimum is applied. Grades are compared within their own scales. Coarse families preserve 5.10 as text, never as the decimal 5.1. Archive-supported resolutions of ambiguous source 5.1 values are documented in the notebook.

Sources & reproducibility

Source code, data definitions, audit tables and the executed notebook are in the public repository. The original tick archive specifies no data license; our MIT code license does not license those original records. The explorer includes route features and aggregate counts only.

CLIMBING + DATA

A quick glossary

Tick / climbing log
A personal route record on Mountain Project. It can describe an ascent or an attempt, not necessarily a successful send. Our counts come from the historical archive.
Sport
Roped climbing with fixed bolts for protection.
Trad (traditional)
Roped climbing where climbers place removable protection, such as cams and nuts. Mixed sport/trad routes count as trad here.
Bouldering
Short climbs without a rope, usually protected with crash pads and spotters. We compare their V grades separately.
Aid
Using gear to support body weight and move upward. Aid, ice and snow routes stay in the source dataset but are excluded from the main rock comparisons.
Pitch / multi-pitch
A pitch is a section of a roped climb between belays. Multi-pitch routes have more than one section.
YDS / grade family
The Yosemite Decimal System grades roped rock climbs. A family combines subgrades: 5.10a–5.10d all enter 5.10.
V scale
The bouldering difficulty scale: V0, V1 and upward. YDS and V grades are not interchangeable.
PG-13, R and X
Optional designations for increasing seriousness of protection and fall consequences. An absent label is not a safety guarantee.
Star rating
A separate opinion of route quality, not difficulty. Quality ratings are separate from our primary popularity measure: recorded ticks.
Sampled climbers
Distinct archived user IDs on a route. Adding these counts across routes counts route-climber pairs, not unique people across the region.
Total / mean / median
Total adds all records. Mean divides ticks by routes. Median is the middle route's count and is less affected by heavily ticked climbs.
Embedding / PCA
A compact view of route features. Nearby points have similar profiles in the two plotted components; PCA retains about 48% of feature variation here.
